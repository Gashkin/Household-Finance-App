# GBD-23 — Cards, loans and mortgages against statements

Source: [GBD-23](https://app.notion.com/41ad3419fef081ef9ad8f917640c5af7). Policy inspected: live Product Build authority, 21 November 2026. Integration base: main d890371 (PR #33). This story adds one complete statement-management journey; it does not initiate payments or lender actions.

## Operator journey

1. In Money position, create an owned DEBT account and an owned CASH repayment account. Debt ledger balances are negative when owed; statement amounts owed are positive. A negative statement balance is a lender credit. Credit limits are descriptive and never available cash.
2. Open **Cards, loans & mortgages**. Add the lender type, contractual monthly payment, monthly fee, maturity (card planning end), allowed immediate extra principal, extra-payment fee, dated annual rates and explicit lender assumptions. Enter a synthetic/manual statement cycle, source, closing amount, due date and minimum payment. Unknown components may remain blank only while incomplete.
3. Complete statements must satisfy opening + purchases/drawdown + interest + fees − repayments = closing. The minimum cannot exceed a positive statement balance. Completing evidence does not reconcile or adjust the ledger. Reconcile the signed closing amount at the cycle end in Money position. The screen separately reports component completeness, historical ledger difference, current booked outstanding and canonical reconciliation status.
4. Next statement adds a later, nonoverlapping cycle. **Correct statement** appends a correction of the latest exact cycle and preserves prior evidence. Term changes retain dated rate history; known changes are appended with today/future effective dates. Audits retain before/after values and the operator's reason.
5. Record both booked settlement legs in Money position and link them in Review & reconcile. Only reciprocal confirmed transfer/card-settlement links from the selected cash account reduce repayment expectations. Pending/unlinked credits never silently settle a statement. Purchases remain spending, settlements do not become a second purchase. Unlinking reopens the reservation; it is capped at current booked debt. Refunds and ledger corrections remain in the existing canonical activity workflow.
6. **Plan ahead → Outlook** includes debt repayment cash debits automatically, including outstanding overdue repayments. Do not enter the same repayment as another bill. These generated expectations are read-only there; use debt terms/statements and actual transfer links to change their evidence. Card planning reserves the remaining full statement balance, not merely its minimum, capped at current debt. The minimum and due date are shown separately with overdue warnings. Loan/mortgage expectations use the dated monthly contractual schedule through maturity, reduced by reviewed actual repayments since the selected statement. Cash-account pending debits are reserved only by the existing baseline calculation.
7. Compare a loan/mortgage's contractual baseline with an immediate extra principal payment. The backend applies the lender allowance, extra fee and payment-account cash after pending debits; overdraft/credit capacity is excluded. Each row shows date, principal, interest, fees, total payment and remaining balance. The statement card separately shows net principal reduction (repayments less statement interest and fees); incomplete components remain unknown. Expand any historical cycle to inspect its original component amounts and due date. Full payoff and maturity balloon are distinct. Reserve shortfalls and incomplete/mismatched/unreconciled evidence remain visible. Comparisons never write financial state.
8. Download the local JSON debt report. It contains generation time, statement/term evidence, account reconciliation warnings and (when displayed) a freshly recomputed comparison from the same read transaction. It contains private financial information: keep it locally. Encrypted workspace backup already includes the owner-scoped debt state, operation receipts and audits in ledger review records; no schema migration or separate debt backup format is needed.

## Calculation boundary

The existing deterministic DebtScenarioService remains authoritative for constant-rate, fee-free full-payoff comparisons. Its monthly half-up interest helper is reused by the dated-rate row model. Rates effective on each payment date are divided by 12. The extra payment applies immediately; its explicit fee is paid from cash. A fixed monthly fee is included where supplied. This is a monthly illustration, not daily lender accrual or a binding payoff quote. Future unknown rates, tax, offset accounts, insurance and unentered lender constraints are excluded and must be disclosed in lender assumptions. Maturity truncates the contractual schedule and any remaining principal is explicitly a balloon; savings then are not a full-payoff comparison. The model supports at most 1,200 payments within safe integer totals.

Statement reconciliation is historical and revision-specific. Later activity can invalidate its canonical reconciliation marker; the statement itself remains durable. Only the latest statement/correction is used for current repayment planning. A lender credit is never reclassified as spendable cash. Incomplete statements remain useful evidence with explicit warnings, rather than fabricated zero interest or fees.

## Access and recovery

Authenticated workspace, CSRF header, strict JSON coercion/unknown-field rejection, capability checks and account ownership apply. Every save has a stable operation UUID and state revision. Identical replay returns existing state without duplicate audit. Changed replay, stale revision and foreign accounts reject before writes. Unknown HTTP outcomes retain the draft and offer operation-status recovery; a known stale save retains input for comparison. Access denial clears private saved and draft data. The existing workspace write guard serializes concurrent mutations and checks encrypted-backup capacity before commit; failure rolls back state, receipt and audit together.
